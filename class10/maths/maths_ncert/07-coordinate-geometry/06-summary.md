## 7.4 Summary

In this chapter, you have studied the following points :

1. The distance between P($x_1$, $y_1$) and Q($x_2$, $y_2$) is $\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.

2. The distance of a point P($x$, $y$) from the origin is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.

3. The coordinates of the point P($x$, $y$) which divides the line segment joining the points A($x_1$, $y_1$) and B($x_2$, $y_2$) internally in the ratio $m_1 : m_2$ are
   $$\left( \dfrac{m_1 x_2 + m_2 x_1}{m_1 + m_2}, \dfrac{m_1 y_2 + m_2 y_1}{m_1 + m_2} \right).$$

4. The mid-point of the line segment joining the points P($x_1$, $y_1$) and Q($x_2$, $y_2$) is $\left( \dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$.

<div class="key-points" markdown="1">

**A note to the reader :** Section 7.3 discusses the Section Formula for the coordinates ($x$, $y$) of a point P which divides **internally** the line segment joining the points A($x_1$, $y_1$) and B($x_2$, $y_2$) in the ratio $m_1 : m_2$. Note that, here, PA : PB = $m_1 : m_2$. However, if P does not lie between A and B but lies on the line AB, outside the line segment AB, and PA : PB = $m_1 : m_2$, we say that P divides **externally** the line segment joining the points A and B. You will study Section Formula for such case in higher classes.
