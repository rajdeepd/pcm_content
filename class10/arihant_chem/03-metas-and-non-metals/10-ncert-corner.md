# NCERT Corner - Metals and Non-metals

## In-Text Questions

**Q1.** Give an example of a metal which
(i) is a liquid at room temperature. **Ans:** Mercury (Hg)
(ii) can be easily cut with a knife. **Ans:** Sodium (Na)
(iii) is the best conductor of heat. **Ans:** Silver (Ag)
(iv) is a poor conductor of heat. **Ans:** Lead (Pb)

**Q2.** Explain the meanings of malleable and ductile.
**Ans:** **Malleable** — can be beaten into thin sheets. **Ductile** — can be drawn into thin wires.

**Q3.** Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?
**Ans:** Sodium is highly reactive and catches fire when exposed to air due to vigorous reaction with oxygen and moisture. Hence it is stored under kerosene oil.

**Q4.** Write equations for the reactions of (i) iron with steam (ii) calcium and potassium with water.
**Ans:**
$$3Fe(s) + 4H_2O(g) \rightarrow Fe_3O_4(s) + 4H_2(g)$$
$$Ca(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow Ca(OH)_2(aq) + H_2(g)$$
$$2K(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2KOH(aq) + H_2(g) + \text{heat}$$

**Q5.** Which gas is produced when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to a reactive metal?
**Ans:** Hydrogen gas ($H_2$).
$$Fe + 2HCl \rightarrow FeCl_2 + H_2$$

**Q6.** What would you observe when zinc is added to a solution of iron(II) sulphate?
**Ans:** Zinc being more reactive displaces iron. The green colour of $FeSO_4$ solution fades and iron is deposited.
$$Zn(s) + FeSO_4(aq) \rightarrow ZnSO_4(aq) + Fe(s)$$

**Q7.** Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?
**Ans:** Due to strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the oppositely charged ions, a considerable amount of energy is required to break the inter-ionic attraction.
