# Physical Properties of Non-metals

Non-metals have properties generally opposite to metals:

## Comparison Table

| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lustre | Shiny (metallic lustre) | Dull (except iodine, diamond) |
| Hardness | Generally hard | Generally soft (except diamond) |
| Malleability | Malleable | Non-malleable (brittle) |
| Ductility | Ductile | Non-ductile |
| Conductivity (heat) | Good conductors | Poor conductors (except graphite) |
| Conductivity (electricity) | Good conductors | Poor conductors (except graphite) |
| Sonority | Sonorous | Non-sonorous |
| State | Mostly solids (except Hg) | Solids, liquids (Br), or gases |
| Melting/Boiling points | Generally high | Generally low (except diamond, graphite) |

{: .note }
> **Exceptions to Physical Properties of Non-metals**
> - **Iodine** is a non-metal but it is lustrous.
> - **Diamond** (an allotrope of carbon) is the hardest natural substance known.
> - **Graphite** (an allotrope of carbon) is a good conductor of electricity.
> - **Bromine** is the only non-metal that is a liquid at room temperature.

## Oxides of Metals and Non-metals

- **Metal oxides** are generally **basic** in nature (some are amphoteric, e.g., $Al_2O_3$, $ZnO$).
- **Non-metal oxides** are generally **acidic** in nature.

$$2Mg(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2MgO(s) \quad \text{(basic oxide)}$$

$$S(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow SO_2(g) \quad \text{(acidic oxide)}$$
