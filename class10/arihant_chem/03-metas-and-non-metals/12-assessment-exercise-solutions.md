## MCQ Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">1. Answer: (b)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Sodium (Na) is soft enough to be cut with a knife, highly reactive to air (stored in kerosene oil), and reacts vigorously with cold water producing NaOH and $H_2$ gas.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">2. Answer: (b)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Silver (Ag) is the least reactive among Mg, Ag, Zn and Cu. Therefore, it can be displaced from its salt solution by all the other three metals which are more reactive than silver.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">3. Answer: (d)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Non-metals generally do not react with water.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">4. Answer: (a)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> During electrolytic refining, the pure metal is always deposited on the cathode. The impure metal dissolves from the anode into the electrolyte, and pure metal from the electrolyte deposits on the cathode.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">5. Answer: (d)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Iron (Fe) is more reactive than copper (Cu). Therefore, Fe can displace Cu from $CuSO_4$ solution.</p>

$$Fe(s) + CuSO_4(aq) \rightarrow FeSO_4(aq) + Cu(s)$$

<p class="answer-text" style="margin-top: -10px;">In other options, the metal added is less reactive than the metal in the salt, so no reaction takes place.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">6. Answer: (b)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> When sulphur is heated in air, it burns to form sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$) gas.</p>

$$S(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow SO_2(g)$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">7. Answer: (d)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Potassium reacts vigorously with water to form KOH and $H_2$. Silver has very low reactivity (below hydrogen in the reactivity series) and does not react with water at all.</p>

$$2K(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2KOH(aq) + H_2(g)$$

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">8. Answer: (b)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Malleability is the property of metals to be beaten into thin sheets. Steel sheets are used to make containers because they can be shaped and moulded into the required form.</p>

## Case Based Question Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">12 (i)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> From the table, <strong>Copper (Cu)</strong> with thermal conductivity 4.01 W/cm-K is the best conductor of heat. The electrolyte used in refining of copper is <strong>acidified copper sulphate solution</strong> ($CuSO_4$).</p>

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">12 (ii)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Metals at the top of the reactivity series (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) cannot be reduced by carbon. They are obtained by <strong>electrolytic reduction</strong> (electrolysis) of their molten salts. Electricity acts as the reducing agent.</p>

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">12 (iii)</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> <strong>Aluminium</strong> forms amphoteric oxide ($Al_2O_3$) which reacts with both acids and bases.</p>

- $Na_2O$ — **Basic oxide** (metal oxide)
- $Al_2O_3$ — **Amphoteric oxide** (reacts with both acids and bases)
- $SO_3$ — **Acidic oxide** (non-metal oxide)

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">12 (iii) Or</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Since the oxide $A_2O_3$ is acidic in nature, element A is a <strong>non-metal</strong>. Non-metal oxides are generally acidic in nature.</p>

## Short Answer Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">16.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> A colour change would be observed only when iron filings are added to <strong>$CuSO_4$ solution</strong> (blue colour fades to green/colourless). Iron is more reactive than copper and displaces it.</p>

$$Fe(s) + CuSO_4(aq) \rightarrow FeSO_4(aq) + Cu(s)$$

<p class="answer-text" style="margin-top: -10px;">Iron cannot displace Mg, Zn or Fe from their sulphate solutions as they are equally or more reactive than iron.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">17.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Iron reacts with oxygen and moisture in the atmosphere to form hydrated iron(III) oxide (rust).</p>

$$4Fe + 3O_2 + 2xH_2O \rightarrow 2Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O \quad (\text{rust})$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Prevention methods:</strong> (i) Painting/oiling/greasing, (ii) Galvanisation (coating with zinc), (iii) Electroplating with chromium/nickel, (iv) Alloying (e.g. stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni).</p>

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">18.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong></p>

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Step 1 — Roasting:</strong> The sulphide ore is heated strongly in excess air to convert it into metal oxide.</p>

$$2XS(s) + 3O_2(g) \xrightarrow{Heat} 2XO(s) + 2SO_2(g)$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Step 2 — Reduction with carbon:</strong> The metal oxide is reduced using carbon (coke) as reducing agent.</p>

$$XO(s) + C(s) \rightarrow X(s) + CO(g)$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Step 3 — Refining:</strong> The impure metal is purified by electrolytic refining.</p>

## Long Answer Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">19.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (i)</p>

| Roasting | Calcination |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ore is heated in excess of air | Ore is heated in limited supply of air |
| Used for sulphide ores | Used for carbonate ores |
| $SO_2$ is produced along with metal oxide | $CO_2$ is produced along with metal oxide |
| e.g. $2ZnS + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2ZnO + 2SO_2$ | e.g. $ZnCO_3 \rightarrow ZnO + CO_2$ |

<p class="answer-text">(ii) (a) Metals in the middle of the reactivity series (e.g. Zn, Fe, Pb) are moderately reactive. Their sulphide/carbonate ores are first converted to oxides by roasting/calcination, then reduced with carbon.</p>

$$ZnO(s) + C(s) \rightarrow Zn(s) + CO(g)$$

<p class="answer-text">(b) Metals high up in the reactivity series (e.g. Na, K, Ca, Al) cannot be reduced by carbon. They are obtained by <strong>electrolytic reduction</strong> of their molten chlorides.</p>

$$\text{At cathode: } Na^+ + e^- \rightarrow Na$$

$$\text{At anode: } 2Cl^- \rightarrow Cl_2 + 2e^-$$

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">20.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Metals are found in both <strong>free state</strong> (Au, Ag, Cu) and <strong>combined state</strong> (as oxides, sulphides, carbonates).</p>

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Reaction with oxygen:</strong> All metals combine with oxygen to form metal oxides.</p>

$$4Na + O_2 \rightarrow 2Na_2O$$

$$2Cu + O_2 \rightarrow 2CuO$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Reaction with water:</strong> Na/K react vigorously with cold water; Mg with hot water; Al/Fe with steam only.</p>

$$2Na(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2NaOH(aq) + H_2(g)$$

$$3Fe(s) + 4H_2O(g) \rightarrow Fe_3O_4(s) + 4H_2(g)$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Reaction with dilute acids:</strong> Metals react with acids to form salt and hydrogen gas.</p>

$$Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow MgCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)$$
