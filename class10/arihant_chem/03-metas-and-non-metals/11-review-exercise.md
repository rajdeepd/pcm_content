## Competency Focused (Objective) Questions

### Multiple Choice Questions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">1. Which of the following is the most reactive metal?</h4>

(a) Iron
(b) Copper
(c) Sodium
(d) Gold

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (c) Sodium is the most reactive metal among the given options. It reacts vigorously with water and catches fire when exposed to air. In the reactivity series, Na is placed much higher than Fe, Cu or Au.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">2. The metal which exists as a liquid at room temperature is:</h4>

(a) Sodium
(b) Mercury
(c) Bromine
(d) Gallium

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (b) Mercury (Hg) is the only metal that exists as a liquid at room temperature. Bromine is a non-metal liquid. Gallium melts at slightly above room temperature (29.76°C).</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">3. An alloy of copper and zinc is called:</h4>

(a) Bronze
(b) Brass
(c) Steel
(d) Solder

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (b) Brass is an alloy of copper (Cu) and zinc (Zn). Bronze is Cu + Sn, Steel is Fe + C, Solder is Pb + Sn.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">4. Which of the following metals does not react with cold or hot water?</h4>

(a) Na
(b) Ca
(c) Mg
(d) Fe

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (d) Iron (Fe) does not react with cold or hot water. It reacts only with steam to form $Fe_3O_4$ and $H_2$. Na reacts vigorously with cold water, Ca reacts with cold water, and Mg reacts with hot water.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">5. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?</h4>

(a) $Na_2O$
(b) $Al_2O_3$
(c) $CO_2$
(d) $SO_2$

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (b) Aluminium oxide ($Al_2O_3$) is an amphoteric oxide as it reacts with both acids and bases to form salt and water.</p>

$$Al_2O_3 + 6HCl \rightarrow 2AlCl_3 + 3H_2O$$

$$Al_2O_3 + 2NaOH \rightarrow 2NaAlO_2 + H_2O$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">6. The process of coating iron with zinc is called:</h4>

(a) Electroplating
(b) Galvanisation
(c) Anodising
(d) Alloying

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (b) Galvanisation is the process of applying a protective zinc coating to iron or steel to prevent rusting. The zinc layer provides a barrier and also acts as a sacrificial anode.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">7. Which metal is used in the thermit reaction for welding of railway tracks?</h4>

(a) Iron
(b) Copper
(c) Aluminium
(d) Zinc

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (c) Aluminium is used as the reducing agent in the thermit reaction.</p>

$$Fe_2O_3(s) + 2Al(s) \rightarrow 2Fe(l) + Al_2O_3(s) + \text{Heat}$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">8. Which of the following is NOT a property of ionic compounds?</h4>

(a) High melting point
(b) Conducts electricity in solid state
(c) Soluble in water
(d) Hard and brittle

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (b) Ionic compounds do NOT conduct electricity in the solid state because the ions are held in fixed positions in the crystal lattice and cannot move freely. They conduct electricity only in molten state or in aqueous solution.</p>

## Short Answer Questions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">9. What is meant by the terms 'roasting' and 'calcination'? Give one example of each.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> <strong>Roasting:</strong> Heating of sulphide ores strongly in the presence of excess air to convert them into metal oxides.</p>

$$2ZnS(s) + 3O_2(g) \xrightarrow{Heat} 2ZnO(s) + 2SO_2(g)$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Calcination:</strong> Heating of carbonate ores strongly in the limited supply of air to convert them into metal oxides.</p>

$$ZnCO_3(s) \xrightarrow{Heat} ZnO(s) + CO_2(g)$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">10. Differentiate between metals and non-metals on the basis of their chemical properties (any 4 points).</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong></p>

| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Oxides | Form basic oxides | Form acidic oxides |
| Reaction with acids | Give salt + $H_2$ | Generally do not react |
| Electron tendency | Lose electrons (electropositive) | Gain electrons (electronegative) |
| Displacement | Displace less reactive metals from salt solutions | Cannot displace metals from salt solutions |

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">11. What is an alloy? What are its advantages? Give examples of two alloys and their compositions.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> An alloy is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal. <strong>Advantages:</strong> Alloys are stronger, more resistant to corrosion, have better electrical/thermal properties, and can have customised melting points compared to pure metals.</p>

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Examples:</strong></p>

- **Brass** = Copper + Zinc (used in decorative items, taps)
- **Stainless Steel** = Iron + Nickel + Chromium (does not rust; used in utensils, cutlery)

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">12. Why is aluminium oxide considered an amphoteric oxide? Write chemical equations to support your answer.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Aluminium oxide is amphoteric because it reacts with both acids and bases to form salt and water.</p>

$$Al_2O_3 + 6HCl \rightarrow 2AlCl_3 + 3H_2O$$

$$Al_2O_3 + 2NaOH \rightarrow 2NaAlO_2 + H_2O$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">13. Why is it not possible to extract sodium or magnesium by reduction with carbon?</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Sodium and magnesium are highly reactive metals placed at the top of the activity series. They have more affinity for oxygen than carbon does. Therefore, carbon cannot reduce their oxides. These metals are obtained by electrolytic reduction of their molten chlorides instead.</p>

## Long Answer Questions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">14. A metal M is found in nature as its carbonate. It is used in galvanisation. Identify M. How is M extracted from its ore? Write the balanced chemical equations involved.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> The metal M is <strong>Zinc (Zn)</strong>, which is found as zinc carbonate ($ZnCO_3$) and is used in galvanisation.</p>

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Step 1 — Calcination:</strong></p>

$$ZnCO_3(s) \xrightarrow{Heat} ZnO(s) + CO_2(g)$$

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Step 2 — Reduction with carbon:</strong></p>

$$ZnO(s) + C(s) \rightarrow Zn(s) + CO(g)$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">15. What are ionic compounds? Explain their properties with examples.</h4>

<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ionic compounds are formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal. e.g. NaCl, $MgCl_2$, CaO.</p>

<p class="answer-text"><strong>Properties:</strong></p>

1. **Physical nature:** Hard solids, brittle.
2. **Melting/Boiling points:** High (e.g. NaCl m.p. = 1074 K).
3. **Solubility:** Soluble in water, insoluble in kerosene/petrol.
4. **Electrical conductivity:** Do not conduct in solid state; conduct in molten state and aqueous solution.
