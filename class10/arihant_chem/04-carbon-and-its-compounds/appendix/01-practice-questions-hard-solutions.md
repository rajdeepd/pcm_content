## MCQ Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H1. Answer: (b)</h4>
<span class="badge-beyond">Beyond NCERT</span>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> 2,4-DNP indicates a carbonyl compound. Tollens’ negative rules out aldehyde; hence ketone → propanone.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H2. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Aldehyde carbon is C-1; longest chain has 4 carbons (butanal) with a methyl at C-3 → 3-methylbutanal.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H3. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Propanal and propanone have the same molecular formula $C_3H_6O$ but different functional groups (aldehyde vs ketone).</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H4. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> $CH_3COOH + NaHCO_3 \rightarrow CH_3COONa + H_2O + CO_2\uparrow$ and $CO_2$ turns lime water milky.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H5. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Conc. $H_2SO_4$ acts as a dehydrating agent and removes water, shifting equilibrium towards ester formation.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H6. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Saponification: ester + NaOH → sodium salt of the acid + alcohol.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H7. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ethanol forms hydrogen bonds; hence it has the highest boiling point among the options.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H8. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Decolourises bromine water → unsaturated. Hydrogenation gives butane → must be a $C_4$ alkene → butene.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H9. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> $Ca^{2+}$/$Mg^{2+}$ ions form insoluble calcium/magnesium salts of fatty acids (scum), so lather is not formed.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H10. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ethanol \(\rightarrow\) ethanal \(\rightarrow\) ethanoic acid on oxidation with acidified dichromate.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H11. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Effervescence with $NaHCO_3$ indicates a carboxylic acid; ester formation with ethanol confirms ethanoic acid.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H12. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Soaps are sodium/potassium salts of long-chain carboxylic acids. Detergents are usually sulphonates/sulphates; biodegradability varies.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H13. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> $CH_3CH=CHCH_3$ has restricted rotation and each double-bond carbon has two different groups → cis–trans isomerism possible.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H14. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Dehydration of ethanol with excess conc. $H_2SO_4$ at about \(170^\circ\text{C}\) gives ethene.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H15. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> $CH_3COCH_2CH_3$ is a 4-carbon ketone → butanone (2-butanone).</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H16. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Soaps form insoluble salts with $Ca^{2+}/Mg^{2+}$ ions in hard water; detergents do not form such scum.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H17. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Addition reaction adds atoms across a multiple bond: $C_2H_4 + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni} C_2H_6$.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H18. Answer: (b)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Homologues differ by \(CH_2\) and have the same functional group: $C_2H_5OH$ and $C_3H_7OH$.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H19. Answer: (a)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Alkanes are least reactive towards oxidation, alcohols oxidise (to aldehydes/acids), and aldehydes oxidise most readily (to acids). Hence alkane &lt; alcohol &lt; aldehyde.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">H20. Answer: (b)</h4>
<span class="badge-beyond">Beyond NCERT</span>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ethanol gives iodoform test because it is oxidised to ethanal which contains the $CH_3CO-$ group.</p>

## Assertion–Reason Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">AR1. Answer: (a)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ethanol is saturated and does not undergo addition reactions, so it does not decolourise bromine water. Both statements are true and R explains A.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">AR2. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Soap solutions are basic (A true) due to hydrolysis of soap (salt of weak acid and strong base). Reason given is false.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">AR3. Answer: (a)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ketones resist oxidation; hence propanone is not easily oxidised compared to aldehydes. Both true and R explains A.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">AR4. Answer: (c)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Ethanoic acid turns blue litmus red (A true). It does not ionise completely (it is a weak acid), so R is false. Hence (c).</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">AR5. Answer: (a)</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Unsaturated hydrocarbons show addition reactions because the \(\pi\)-bond is weaker and breaks to form new \(\sigma\)-bonds. Both true and R explains A.</p>

## Short Answer Solutions

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">SA1.</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong></p>

$$C_2H_5OH + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2CO_2 + 3H_2O$$

$$CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{conc.\ H_2SO_4} CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O$$

$$CH_3COOH + NaHCO_3 \rightarrow CH_3COONa + H_2O + CO_2\uparrow$$

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">SA2.</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> Any two tests:</p>

- **With $NaHCO_3$**: ethanoic acid → brisk effervescence ($CO_2$); ethanol → no effervescence.  
- **With blue litmus**: ethanoic acid turns it red; ethanol shows no change.

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">SA3.</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> In hard water, $Ca^{2+}$/$Mg^{2+}$ ions react with soap (fatty acid salts) to form insoluble scum. Soap is consumed in scum formation, so lather/cleansing reduces.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">SA4.</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> (i) Forming \(C^{4+}\) requires very high ionisation energy (loss of 4 electrons). (ii) Forming \(C^{4-}\) is unstable due to strong electron–electron repulsion and low electron gain enthalpy. Therefore carbon shares electrons forming covalent bonds.</p>

---

<h4 style="text-transform: none;">SA5.</h4>
<p class="answer-text"><strong class="sol-label">Sol.</strong> A sooty flame indicates a high percentage of carbon and incomplete combustion (often unsaturated/aromatic hydrocarbons). Example: ethene/benzene burns with a sooty flame.</p>
